## Part 2: Answer Key & Full Explanations

### Question 1 — Answer: ✅ (A) adopt

**Why (A) is correct:**
The clue word **"shelter"** (a place for homeless animals) combined with "loves animals" points to the action of taking an animal home to care for it. "**Adopt**" is the exact verb used for this action ("adopt a pet" = לאמץ חיית מחמד).

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(B) invent** = להמציא. You don't invent a living animal – you invent objects or ideas.
- **(C) destroy** = להרוס. Contradicts "loves animals."
- **(D) forget** = לשכוח. Grammatically odd ("forget a puppy from the shelter" makes no sense) and contradicts the context.

**💡 Learning tip:** "Adopt" is a high-frequency word in academic and everyday English – it can also mean "to accept and use" (e.g., "The company adopted a new policy").

---

### Question 2 — Answer: ✅ (C) quiet

**Why (C) is correct:**
The clue is **"people go there to read and study quietly"** — this is a direct definition clue. A place where people need quiet is naturally described as "**quiet**."

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) loud** = רועש. The **opposite** of what is described. This is the classic "trap answer" that tests if you read carefully.
- **(B) crowded** = צפוף. Could be true sometimes, but it doesn't match "read and study quietly." A crowded place could still be quiet, but that's not what the sentence emphasizes.
- **(D) colorful** = צבעוני. Irrelevant to the context.

**💡 Learning tip:** When the sentence gives you a direct description after the blank ("people go there to..."), that description IS the clue. Don't overthink it.

---

### Question 3 — Answer: ✅ (B) wet

**Why (B) is correct:**
**Logical cause-and-effect:** no umbrella + rain → you become **wet** (רטוב). This is the direct physical consequence.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) cold** = קר. Possible in some cases (cold rain) but "wet" is the direct and inevitable result. The sentence doesn't mention temperature.
- **(C) hungry** = רעב. Completely unrelated to rain.
- **(D) tired** = עייף. Completely unrelated to rain.

**💡 Learning tip:** Look for the MOST DIRECT consequence. Rain → wet. Heat → hot. Hard work → tired. The obvious answer is usually correct at the Easy level.

---

### Question 4 — Answer: ✅ (B) because

**Why (B) is correct:**
This is a **cause-and-effect** relationship. First part: "Tom studies hard." Second part: "he wants to pass the final exam" — this is the REASON for studying hard. "**Because**" introduces a reason.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) but** = אבל. This shows contrast, not cause. There's no contrast here — both facts are consistent.
- **(C) although** = למרות ש-. Also shows contrast. Wanting to pass is not the opposite of studying hard.
- **(D) or** = או. Shows alternatives, but the sentence is presenting a single logical flow.

**💡 Learning tip:** Memorize the "logic words":
- Cause → because, since, as
- Contrast → but, however, although, despite
- Addition → and, moreover, also
- Result → so, therefore, thus

---

### Question 5 — Answer: ✅ (C) excited

**Why (C) is correct:**
A **surprise birthday cake** is a joyful event. Children seeing it would naturally feel **excited** (נרגשים, שמחים) — a positive emotion.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) sad** = עצובים. Opposite of a happy event.
- **(B) bored** = משועממים. Also opposite of "excited" about a surprise.
- **(D) angry** = כועסים. No reason to be angry about a cake.

**💡 Learning tip:** Emotions should match the context. Birthday → happy/excited/surprised. Funeral → sad/upset. Exam → nervous/worried. Learn to pair emotions with typical situations.

---

### Question 6 — Answer: ✅ (A) help

**Why (A) is correct:**
The request "can you please ______ me?" after saying the problem is difficult is a natural request for assistance. "**Help**" fits perfectly.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(B) hurt** = לפגוע. Why would you ask someone to hurt you? Contradicts the request context.
- **(C) ignore** = להתעלם. Opposite of asking for assistance.
- **(D) avoid** = להימנע. Grammatically works but doesn't match the context of seeking support.

**💡 Learning tip:** "Can you please help me?" is one of the most common English phrases. Recognizing formulaic expressions helps you answer quickly.

---

### Question 7 — Answer: ✅ (B) fluently

**Why (B) is correct:**
The sentence mentions she speaks **three languages** and lists them (English, Spanish, French). The context suggests high ability. "**Fluently**" (ברהיטות) describes speaking a language well.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) poorly** = בצורה גרועה. Contradicts the impressive fact of speaking three languages.
- **(C) rarely** = לעיתים רחוקות. Refers to frequency, not skill. You wouldn't usually describe three languages you speak as "rarely spoken."
- **(D) silently** = בשקט, ללא קול. Illogical – you can't speak languages silently.

**💡 Learning tip:** "Fluent/fluently" is a key word for describing language proficiency – essential vocabulary for AMIRNET.

---

### Question 8 — Answer: ✅ (A) turn off

**Why (A) is correct:**
Before leaving a room, the logical action regarding lights is to **turn them off** (לכבות). This is a very common phrasal verb.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(B) turn on** = להדליק. Opposite – you don't turn lights on when leaving.
- **(C) grow up** = לגדול (to become adult). Grammatically and semantically wrong.
- **(D) look after** = לטפל ב-. You don't "look after" lights.

**💡 Learning tip:** Phrasal verbs are very common in AMIRNET. Learn the most common ones: turn on/off, turn up/down, pick up, take off, put on, give up, look after.

---

### Question 9 — Answer: ✅ (B) drink

**Why (B) is correct:**
The natural collocation with "water" is "**drink water**." This is a basic, fixed phrase in English.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) eat** = לאכול. You don't "eat" water. (Eat food, drink liquids.)
- **(C) cook** = לבשל. You don't cook water in the context of a health recommendation.
- **(D) throw** = לזרוק. Illogical.

**💡 Learning tip:** Verb-noun collocations are crucial: drink water/coffee, eat food/bread, wear clothes/shoes, take medicine/photos, make decisions/mistakes, do homework/business.

---

### Question 10 — Answer: ✅ (C) taller

**Why (C) is correct:**
The sentence provides measurements: 185 cm vs. 170 cm. The comparative for height is "**taller**" (גבוה יותר). 185 > 170, so the brother is **taller**.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **(A) shorter** = נמוך יותר. Opposite – the brother is 185 cm (more than 170 cm).
- **(B) smaller** = קטן יותר. "Smaller" usually refers to size/volume, not height specifically. 185 cm is not "smaller."
- **(D) younger** = צעיר יותר. The sentence gives heights, not ages. This is a trap for readers who don't check what the numbers represent.

**💡 Learning tip:** Always check what the evidence in the sentence actually measures. Numbers could refer to height, age, weight, temperature, price, etc.
